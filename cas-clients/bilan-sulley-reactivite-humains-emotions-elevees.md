# BILAN SULLEY

## PROBLÉMATIQUES

- Reactivity towards humans indoors: barking, charging, rapidly wagging tail
- Uncontrolled excitement indoors — difficulty self-regulating
- Unpredictable reactivity towards humans outdoors (joggers, cyclists, pedestrians)
- Reactivity towards other dogs driven by frustration
- Strong leash pulling
- High predatory drive towards cats

## BILAN

Sulley is a 2.5-year-old Spaniel who operates at a constantly elevated emotional state. Indoors, he reacts to humans entering the home with barking, charging, and rapid tail wagging — signs of high arousal combined with a need to control access to his territory. Outdoors, his reactivity towards humans is unpredictable, though no reactions were observed during the assessment, which surprised his owners. He also shows frustration-based reactivity towards other dogs and significant leash pulling. His predatory drive towards cats is notably high.

## AXE DE TRAVAIL

- Teaching Sulley to self-regulate and lower his emotional baseline
- Developing the ability to observe without reacting
- Increasing physical and mental stimulation
- Managing reactivity towards humans (indoors and outdoors)
- Managing frustration-based reactivity towards other dogs
- Leash walking

---

## Bilan S1

### CONTEXTE / OBJECTIF DU COURS

Work on two areas: prey drive (recall and stay with toys) and managing reactivity when humans arrive at home, starting with the doorbell.

### DÉROULÉ DU COURS

1. Recall and stay exercises with toys to channel prey drive.
2. First attempt: Sulley in his basket with the doorbell triggered. Failure — persistent barking despite management attempts.
3. Reversed approach: take Sulley outside, show him the doorbell being pressed, reward calm behaviour upon hearing the sound from outside.
4. Gradual introduction: bring Sulley back inside, begin closing the door. Very good response from the dog — clear understanding of the exercise mechanics.

### PROGRESSION / RÉSULTATS

The first approach (basket + doorbell) did not work. The reversed approach, starting from outside, yielded very good results. Sulley understood the exercise mechanics well.

Sulley is constantly running at a high emotional level, which demands significant resources for every exercise. Visible signs of rapid fatigue: tongue hanging out, high water intake, with no particular physical effort involved. His workload per session should remain lower than that of a typical dog.

### EXERCICES À TRAVAILLER

- Doorbell exercise (reversed approach): take Sulley outside, trigger the doorbell from outside, reward calm on the sound. Gradually reintroduce going back inside with the door closing. To be practised 5 to 10 minutes per day, several times a day if possible.

---

## Bilan S2

### CONTEXTE / OBJECTIF DU COURS

Work on olfactory search to provide mental stimulation and calm the dog. Introduction of impulse control around predatory triggers near the fence and door training.

### DÉROULÉ DU COURS

- Olfactory search exercises to add mental enrichment and help the dog settle
- Predatory drive work near the fence: "no" marker + redirection onto a toy
- Door training protocol: sit with door open → doorbell rings → door closes → doorbell rings again. Goal: hear the doorbell without barking or becoming overexcited

### PROGRESSION / RÉSULTATS

Sulley engaged with all three areas of work during this session. The door protocol and prey drive redirection were introduced as foundational exercises to be built upon in future sessions.

### EXERCICES À TRAVAILLER

- Olfactory search: hide treats or kibble around the home for Sulley to find independently. Start in a small area and gradually increase the search zone.
- Predatory drive near the fence: if Sulley fixates on something through the fence, say "no" calmly and redirect his attention onto a toy. Reward the redirection.
- Door training: ask Sulley to sit with the door open. Ring the doorbell, then immediately close the door before he can react. Reward calm behavior. Gradually work toward him remaining seated and calm when the bell rings without closing the door.

---

## Bilan S3

### CONTEXTE / OBJECTIF DU COURS

Review and consolidation of olfactory search and doorbell exercises to build reliability. Session also used to assess Sulley's overall physical and emotional state and adjust recommendations accordingly.

### DÉROULÉ DU COURS

- Olfactory search: revisited to reinforce the behavior and improve performance
- Doorbell exercises: repeated to build consistency and reduce reactivity to the sound

### PROGRESSION / RÉSULTATS

Sulley is showing progress on the exercises introduced in S2. However, he is in a state of physical overload — he needs significantly more physical exercise to be calmer and more relaxed at home. He is a dog who escalates quickly under pressure; the work ahead will focus on teaching him to come back down progressively.

Note: Sulley currently has too many toys available, which reduces their value. All interactive toys (balls, ropes) should be removed. Only a few occupation toys (chews, lick mats) should remain accessible.

### EXERCICES À TRAVAILLER

- Olfactory search: continue daily sessions at home, gradually increasing duration and difficulty.
- Doorbell training: continue the protocol from S2. Once Sulley is reliably calm with the door closing after the bell, begin practicing with the door staying open. Always reward calm behavior.
- Increase physical exercise: add regular walks or active outings to help reduce his baseline pressure level.
- Toy management: remove all interactive toys (balls, ropes). Keep only occupation toys (chews, lick mats). Reintroduce interactive toys progressively as rewards during training sessions only.
